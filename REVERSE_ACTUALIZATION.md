## The scene

A fisherman sees another boat turn around to follow. **No agreement is made.** The observing fisherman:

1. Iterates the state of the connection with that boat (intracell)
2. Simulates what he thought was the other boat's happenings
3. Infers from experience
4. Puts himself in the other boat's position
5. Asks backward: "why did I just turn around?"
6. Works backward through reverse actualization until he unlocks the **first-principle keystone** — the verifiable condition that, if false, would falsify the observed behavior

## The doctrine

**Reverse actualization**: work backward from observed behavior to first-principle keystones.

This is different from:
- Forward planning (decide → act)
- Predictive coding (predict → compare → update)
- Reverse actualization: see → infer backward → unlock the first principle

## Why it's novel

The observer doesn't have access to the other's internal state. They have:
- A signal (the boat turned around)
- An experience base (years of seeing boats turn around)
- An imagination (putting themselves in the other boat's position)
- A first-principle keystone (the verifiable condition, e.g. "bathy chart showed structure there")

They work backward from signal through experience + imagination to keystone. The keystone is **falsifiable** — if the bathy chart didn't show structure, the turn-around wouldn't have happened. The keystone IS the receipt.
